# Changelog

Every build that has gone out, newest first. Version 1.0 is in App Store review; the build number
beside it is the TestFlight build, which is where the work lands first.

<div class="release current">
<div class="release-head"><span class="release-version">1.0.0</span> <span class="release-build">202608012324</span> <span class="tag">Latest</span> <span class="release-date">1 August 2026</span></div>
<ul>
<li><strong>A curated list of real places.</strong> Suggested venues now appear when you fill in "Where?" — real named places, not just "Restaurant". The list is edited live rather than compiled into the app, so somewhere that closes is a correction, not an App Store submission.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="release">
<div class="release-head"><span class="release-version">1.0.0</span> <span class="release-build">202608012232</span> <span class="release-date">1 August 2026</span></div>
<ul>
<li><strong>Notifications you control.</strong> Five kinds, each with its own switch. Nothing is muted for you and nothing is switched on behind your back.</li>
<li><strong>Two notifications that were missing.</strong> You now hear when a plan is called off and why, and you get asked afterwards whether a plan actually happened.</li>
<li><strong>A weekly nudge</strong> naming the one group that has gone longest without a plan — never more than a single message, and silent when something is already booked.</li>
<li><strong>Location, scoped to one plan.</strong> Share where you are for the hours around a plan you both agreed to. One point per tap rather than a trail, and it is deleted when the plan is over.</li>
<li><strong>The app badge counted the wrong things</strong> — it missed exactly the plans that were mid-negotiation and waiting on you.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="release">
<div class="release-head"><span class="release-version">1.0.0</span> <span class="release-build">202608011143 → 202608011933</span> <span class="release-date">1 August 2026</span></div>
<ul>
<li><strong>Plans can carry something riding on them.</strong> Both people stake points on a plan happening, and following through pays it back. Never one at the other's expense.</li>
<li><strong>Invite someone to a single plan.</strong> A one-off code that admits them to that plan and nothing else — not to your groups, not to anything after it.</li>
<li><strong>A reliability score</strong>, worked out from plans that happened rather than stored as a verdict. Deliberately not shown inside a couple.</li>
<li><strong>Counter-suggestions move the plan.</strong> Suggesting "Sunday instead?" now carries the new time, so accepting it actually reschedules rather than leaving the old date in place.</li>
<li><strong>The home, calendar and activity screens</strong> no longer flash an empty state before their content arrives.</li>
<li><strong>Plan detail was cramped and jumped as it loaded.</strong> Rebuilt so it settles in one pass.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="release">
<div class="release-head"><span class="release-version">1.0.0</span> <span class="release-build">202607311307 → 202607312105</span> <span class="release-date">31 July 2026</span></div>
<ul>
<li><strong>"Did it happen?"</strong> — the app asks after a plan, and that answer is what everything about reliability is built on.</li>
<li><strong>Cancelling records why</strong> instead of quietly deleting the plan, so "called off three times running" can eventually mean something.</li>
<li><strong>Calendar clash checks.</strong> A proposed time is compared against your calendar on the device. Read-only, opt-in, and nothing about your events is uploaded.</li>
<li><strong>Progress survives a new phone.</strong> Achievements and history are backed up, not just the numbers on top of them.</li>
<li><strong>Levels and goals per activity</strong> — progress in cooking is not progress in travel.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<div class="release">
<div class="release-head"><span class="release-version">1.0.0</span> <span class="release-build">202607302101 → 202607310115</span> <span class="release-date">30 July 2026</span></div>
<ul>
<li><strong>Named groups, each with its own invite code.</strong> With more than one group the app could previously show the code for the wrong one.</li>
<li><strong>Dating and Chill</strong> joined the plan categories, with a safeguard so a category added later can never make a plan silently disappear on an older version of the app.</li>
<li><strong>One-tap templates</strong> for the plans people make constantly — what are we watching, what are we eating.</li>
<li><strong>Saved plans are findable again</strong>, and a plan can carry a place that opens in Maps.</li>
</ul>
</div>
